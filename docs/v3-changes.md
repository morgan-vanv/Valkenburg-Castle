# What changed in V3: from Hobart's Revenge to The Heir's Return

V3 rebuilt the game as a turn-based extraction crawler, following the V3 Game Design Document (https://claude.ai/artifact/Q7766w5bBz11paXkw4YPf1). It launched as "Hobart's Revenge" (V3.0) and became "The Heir's Return" in V3.5. How the game works today: docs/current-rules.md. Rules with numbers, version by version: docs/v3-spec.md. V2 is in `main`'s history (last V2 commit 7a62311).

**Where it stands (V3.9.4, 2026-10-26):** Keven, heir of the late Lord Hobart, camps in Lowgate and wins the village back with story contracts; squads of up to 4 from eight classes delve a three-level castle (the Ruined Keep, the Crossing, the Hollow and its Dragon) on a 20-round lantern. Play is entirely turn-based. Stealth runs on facing and fields of view (V4.0: monsters see by their mood, heroes and crews are blind in a cone behind), noise and hearing; free swings both ways; the Sapper reshapes the level; rival crews parley, fight, rob and drag heroes off. Shelved: free exploration outside combat (docs/shelved/exploration-mode.md) and the Face command.

## Since launch

- **V4.1, gold, gifts and Ilse (2026-10-27):** **Jobs that win something back pay no gold any more.** Rescuing a villager, recovering the copper or the cask book, or bringing back one of your own from a crew pays nothing; instead, the first time each villager comes home they bring a gift: Brother Aldric two potions, Old Wim a round of Hobart's Reserve for everyone on your roster (drunk as they next go in), Gerda a free shield at the forge, Ilse two antidotes. The job card shows the gift where the gold was. **Regular jobs pay more:** rescues 110 / 200 / 280, recoveries 140 / 250 / 340 (plus the heirloom), the Dragon 600. **You start with 120 gold**, and on your very first delve the orc guarding Aldric in the chapel is dozing: a chance to learn to sneak and backstab. **Ilse the alchemist** is a new villager: once Aldric is home and word has spread, he asks you to fetch her from the archive on Level 2, where she has lived among the shelves beside the spider. Smoke and glue bombs, sleep and venom darts and oil are hers now; Mott keeps torches, potions, antidotes, rope, firecrackers and the blasting charge (now 45), and no longer sells the night-eye draught. **Villagers work together:** with Wim and Ilse home, three new brews at the Flagon (Night-eye Stout, Spider-milk Stout, and Dragonfire Porter, which takes 2 off fire, burning, dragon breath and blasts); with Gerda and Ilse, blasting charges at the forge for 30 and the Sapper's **tripwire** (a charge that goes off the moment an enemy steps next to it, or when its fuse runs out); with Aldric and Ilse, the **greater potion** (35, heals 8) and antidotes for 12; with Aldric and Wim, a **hot supper**: everyone who gets out heals 2.

- **V4.1, other ways in (2026-10-26):** the Lantern Sisters never come in by the way you did. Go in by the sally port or the east breach and they may come through the main gate behind you.

- **V4.0, play-test fixes 2 (2026-10-26):** **The Sapper** is a breacher, not a second Sellsword: no more Brace, and a little lighter (HP 9, Might 60, no armor; the Sellsword keeps 10, 70 and his armor). Sappers you already have come down to the new numbers (a wounded one keeps their wounds), and so do the crews' Sappers. **Free kit grows with the hero:** a Sapper brings one improvised explosive a level each delve (a new Sapper brings 1, not 3), a Burglar one improvised pick a level, and each Pick uses one up; the card's kit mark shows how many are left, and Lowgate's Outfit says "Brings their own: 2 improvised picks". Crews follow the same rule. **Picking a target is clear:** with an action armed, the option under the mouse gets a bright white ring with a coloured halo, its tag lights up and the others dim; a jump or shove shows where it lands, a throw or fireball the area it hits. The list above the bar marks its target on the board when you hover an entry, says which way each same-named one is ("Orc · east"), and Tab or the arrows step through the options, Enter takes the marked one. **The hotbar shows only what the hero can do now:** no dimmed Attack, no Set charge without charges; with nothing to do it says "Nothing to do here. Move, or End turn." and the name's hover lists what they can do elsewhere. The bar never scrolls (labels fold to icons if it would). Pack items say "Click to use (key H)" instead of the old "Items ›" menu path. **The Set out sketch is bigger:** it fills the column, names grow with it, and the lantern choice sits under the plan.

- **V4.0, venom (2026-10-26):** spiders' bites are venomous now, on every level. A hero who is bitten (or pricked by a venom dart, or who steps on one of the two new venom traps on Level 2, ringed in green once spotted) is **envenomed**: a green drop with a number appears on their card, the turns until they are **paralyzed**. Paralyzed, they can't move or act for 6 turns, though they don't bleed and aren't down; hits still hurt, and monsters still come for them. Cure it with an **antidote** (Mott always has them, 20 gold: drink it, or give it to a friend next to you), with the Hedgewitch's **Mend**, or by getting out of the castle. Or carry them: next to a paralyzed friend, **Shoulder** lights up; they come along on every move (you move 3 at most), and if you leave by an exit they come out with you. A paralyzed hero left inside when the delve ends is lost. Mott also sells **venom darts** (30 gold): a silent Finesse throw that envenoms a monster or a crew member, who locks up three turns later. The Gallows Choir's Little Mags carries one too, and saves it for a hero standing alone.

- **V4.0, taken only when alone (2026-10-26):** being carried off by a crew is now the rare, nasty surprise, not the usual end of a crew fight. A crew takes a hero only when they are **helpless and alone**: downed, or knocked out by a crew Burglar's sleep dart, with no friend on their feet within 4 hexes. Stay together and a crew that downs one of you finishes them instead if nobody is standing over them; stand next to the crew member and it fights you rather than finish your friend. Wander off alone and a Burglar's sleep dart may find you first: seized while out cold, you wake in their grip being hauled toward the way out, and can try to break free (2 Might slots, 1 hit) or fight back, while the others come running. A lone Keven can be taken too, and that still ends the campaign. And a paid "Open a lock" job that ends with a jammed lock is just a failed job now: the crew keeps your money.

- **V4.0, the board is the screen (UI, 2026-10-26):** the in-castle HUD is rebuilt in Lowgate's look. Along the top: **the lantern** (its stage, a short line such as "daylight: shut it to save oil", the round, and a notched bar of 20 in its bright, dim and dark bands; click it or press L to shut or open it, hover for the numbers), **the job** (what, where, "not yet seen" or "in sight", and a compass arrow once you know where it is; after the prize is in hand, the way out), **Carrying** (what the party carries, e.g. "100 · 45 gold, a garnet": a crew's toll is half of it, and hired crews may turn on a party carrying 150 or more), the **levels** by name once found ("Keep", then "?" until you find the next), and the menu. Down the left, **one compact card per hero**: name, two turn pips (move, action), HP, small icons for the lantern, sneaking, a monster meaning to strike them, an exposed back, standing by a drop, braced or warded, the dark; the pack as four tiny slots, mana as diamonds, and a Burglar's or Sapper's own kit. A downed hero's card says how long they have and what saves them. On the right, the **Chronicle**: the last line or two, or open (it remembers) every line of the delve by round and by "your turn" / "the castle", each with a small icon. At the bottom, **one hotbar row for the selected hero**: Walk/Sneak for that hero, their actions as square buttons with their keys (Attack always there, dimmed with the reason when nobody is in reach; stairs, the way out, doors, chests, loot and the rest glow when they're in reach), their **pack** (click an item to use it: light the torch, drink the potion, throw the smoke bomb), **More** for the rare free things (Give, Drop, Pass the lantern, the shutter, Parley), and **End turn** at the right with a dot for each hero who can still act. Pointing at things answers right there: a **card beside the figure or hex** in three lines (a monster's mood, what it will do, and whether it would see the hero you have selected where they stand), the floor a monster sees **shaded while you hover it** (bright ahead, faint at an unaware one's sides, nothing behind), the move preview in words ("Walk here · 3 of 4 moves", "The orc will see you, then Fenna", "May wake the rats, 1 in 2", "Sneak (S) and it won't, from there."). Hold **Alt** for the full rules and numbers (or turn on "Full hover cards" in the menu for touch screens). Marks over figures are smaller: an enemy shows its mood (z, ?, !), an icon for what it will do (no damage numbers) and its health; heroes show only their health. Attacking: the **target card** says what will happen ("Kills it about half the time"), and the **roll panel** sits low, out of the way: a hex for each try showing its chance, Focus as diamonds you click (each makes a try sure, and the bar shows the kill chance going up: "Was 56% before Focus"), an outcome bar (misses, hurts it, kills it), the noise in words, and Details for the per-hit table. **The castle's turn** gets a thin banner, a caption beside each enemy as it acts (its dice, the damage, "Corin goes down."), and the bar folds to "The castle's turn · 1 of 3 moves · Hold Space to hurry". The **parley card** shows the crew as hexes, what you carry and how they take it, the deals as tiles with the exact payment ("You'd hand over 45 gold and the garnet") and the betrayal warning. Back in Lowgate, a **return report** replaces the old summary: the job's headline, rounds and lantern left, renown and what it opened, then what came out (bounty, loose gold, what goes to the cart, what was lost inside), how each hero came back (HP, XP, level-ups, the fallen and the taken), and what the castle now remembers (rooms mapped, ways in found). On the first delve, three **coach marks** show what to do (move, pick an action, End turn) and step aside for good once you've done it; the menu can turn them off or show them again. The menu also holds the keys and a legend of every mark and icon. Every key works as before.

- **V4.0, crews play by your rules (2026-10-26):** rival crews can now do what your heroes do, and you can watch them do it. They walk 4 (their Burglar 5) or sneak 2 (Burglar 4) and dash like heroes; a sneaking figure, crew or hero, is drawn crouched. Walking, their footsteps are heard and they can wake sleepers; sneaking, they're silent, and the Lantern Sisters sneak past sleeping and unaware monsters and near your party ("Sister Brisk sneaks past the sleeping rats."). Monsters see crews exactly as they see you and go for whoever is nearest, so an orc may fall on the Sisters instead of you; crews fight back. Locked chests aren't free any more: the Burglar sister picks them (silent, or a clunk you may hear: "Something forcing a lock to the north"). Crews spring traps, spot them, walk round them, and their Burglar disarms one in her way. A crew Burglar who gets behind a hero who hasn't seen her backstabs for double damage, and any crew member who strikes a hero who hasn't seen them gets an extra slot. Crew Sappers carry improvised explosives like yours, dig through cracked walls and blast locked doors when it saves them a long walk. New intents show what they plan: 🔓 pick, 🔨 smash, ✂ disarm, ⛏ dig.

- **V4.0, monsters see by their mood (2026-10-26):** the mark over a monster's head now tells you what it sees. **At its post or on patrol** (no mark, or 👣) it sees ahead as before; at its sides it only catches someone walking within 2 hexes or anyone right beside it, and straight behind it only hears someone walking right up. A sneaking Burglar can slip right up beside it unseen and backstab. **Searching** (?), after it heard something, it also sees its sides properly. **Hunting** (!) it keeps track of whoever it has seen, and sees new faces ahead and at its sides. Monsters look whenever anything moves, not only at the hero who is walking: carry the lantern past a sneaking friend and you may light them up; open the lantern next to one and the same. And a monster that spots one hero turns to them and looks again, so a careless walker can give away the friend sneaking nearby. The move preview shows all of it: "Orc will see you, then Fenna". The orc's bellow and everything else about noise is unchanged.

- **V4.0, the Burglar's stride and the heroes' own kit (2026-10-26):** the Burglar now walks 5 hexes (and dashes 5) while still sneaking 4; carrying the prisoner or something heavy still holds them to 3. Rival crews still move 4. The Sapper's free charges are now **Improvised explosives**, their own item with its own icon: 3 every delve, no pack slot, the same blast as a bought blasting charge, used before any bought ones. The Sapper's explosives and the Burglar's improvised pick belong to the hero: they can't be dropped, handed over, stolen by a cutpurse, robbed by a crew, paid to a crew or taken home, and they go with a hero who dies instead of falling on the floor. On the party card they sit apart from the four pack slots, after a small "kit" mark ("Fenna's own: improvised pick. Doesn't take a slot; can't be given away."), and the free-slot count ignores them; in Lowgate's Outfit step the hero's card says "Brings their own: ..." under the slots.

- **V4.0, play-test fixes (2026-10-26):** Walk and Sneak are set per hero: each keeps their own (Burglars start sneaking, everyone else walks), S or the Walk/Sneak buttons change only the selected hero, and a hero set to sneak shows a "Sneak" badge on their card. Monsters notice you the moment it happens, not at the end of the turn: walk through an awake enemy's view and it spots you on that hex, with its "!" and log line right then (an orc bellows right then too), its intent updates at once, and your hero still finishes the move. The move preview warns about every hex of the path, not only where you stop. A monster busy fighting no longer spins round toward whoever steps up behind it or toward footsteps; a hit that lands turns it to face the attacker (a backstab still lands first), a miss doesn't. Crews turn when hit too.

- **V4.0, Lowgate in four steps (UI):** the camp's eight tabs are gone. Every delve now walks one path along the top: **1 Job, 2 Party, 3 Outfit, 4 Set out**, each step clickable at any time, with what you chose shown under its name. Gold is the only number in the header. The primary button is always bottom-right ("Next: Party →" … "Delve"); along the bottom left runs Lowgate itself: three houses (the chapel, the Gilded Flagon, the forge) that are boarded up, carry a red seal when their plea is on the notice post (click it to choose that job), and light up when their people come home, plus Keven's cart and the graves. Jobs are cards: village pleas first with their seal and what they win back, risks as small tags, "Bring back" jobs with the prisoner on the card. Party: four slots with Keven in the first (he can be left out; a party can go in without him), your roster on a bench, a "This party can" strip (fight up close, rally, shoot, slip past unseen, pick locks, heal, blast, burn), and recruits hired straight into a free slot. Recruit cards show what the hire is for ("Best at"), their abilities by name and their HP; the numbers are on hover. Wounded heroes are healed in their party slot (Brother Aldric, once home) or drink a potion there; the infirmary page is gone. Outfit: one card per hero with their four pack slots; Mott's wagon and Keven's cart as tabs beside them. Buying puts the item straight into the selected hero's pack. The cart keeps gear and valuables apart, and Sell all sells only the valuables. Once Gerda is home, blades, mail and shields are bought on each hero's card; once the copper is home, "Pour one" picks an ale. Services you haven't won back stay out of sight instead of showing locks. Set out: the ways in you know (on a new campaign, only the main gate), the lantern shutter, and the plan at a glance. The castle map gives nothing away: you see a sketch only of what you've explored. "Same as last time" takes your last party, packs and way in onto the chosen job and goes straight to Set out. When someone comes home, a banner says so ("Brother Aldric is home.") and "Show me" takes you to where their service now lives. The graves are headstones with a candle each. A welcome card explains the four steps on the first delve.
- **V4.0, going in blind (2026-10-26):** a new campaign goes into the castle by the main gate only. The sally port is barred: its key lies somewhere in the library (or blow the gate with a charge), and carrying the key out counts too. The east breach is choked with rubble: blast it or dig it out. Once open, either way stays open for the rest of the campaign, in and out. The Lantern Sisters make their own entrance: by the sally port with a key of their own, barring the gate again behind them (kill the Burglar sister and the key is yours to use), or by blasting the breach, which you'll hear. Exits behind a shut gate or rubble are not offered as a way out. The camp remembers every hex you've explored, for the Set out map. Story contracts no longer repeat "Story" and "Unlocks" in their warnings. Old saves keep all three entrances open.

- **V3.9.4, tidy-up (2026-10-26):** the move preview's warnings over enemies (free swing, may wake, will notice) and the Burglar's backstab dagger now appear in place instead of sliding in from the top-left corner of the screen each time you hover a hex. Help texts catch up with V3.9.2: heroes are blind only straight behind, backstab works from behind (a monster's back half, straight behind a crew member), the shelved F key is gone from the key list, and the "In the dark" tooltip gives the right notice ranges. Docs, readme and old checks brought up to date for V4.0.

- **V3.9.3, hearing (2026-10-26):** your heroes hear what they can't see. In the castle's turn, footsteps of unseen monsters and crews within earshot leave a trail of faint footprints (closed doors muffle; rats and spiders are quiet, the Dragon carries far), and fights, shouts, smashes, blasts, roars and screams out of sight show as a pulsing ring with an icon. The log sums it up in a line or two ("Footsteps beyond the door to the north-east", "A clash to the west"). Hearing tells you where, not who: heard enemies stay unseen. The marks last until the castle moves again. Monsters gain nothing from it. Also: a monster's shove or drag arrow is no longer drawn once its victim has moved away from it (the intent tag still says what it planned).

- **V3.9.2, turning and blind spots (2026-10-26):** heroes, monsters and crews turn toward whatever they attack, throw at, cast on or use, as well as facing the way they move. Heroes and rival crews are now blind only in a narrow cone straight behind them; monsters keep their whole back half blind, so sneaking round a monster is easier than round a crew. The Burglar's backstab works from any of a monster's back hexes, but only from straight behind a crew member.

- **V3.9.1, simpler facing (2026-10-25 late night):** no more Face button: a hero simply faces the way they last moved, and turning means moving. Front-half sight, blindsided hits and the Burglar's backstab stay. The move preview shows a small arrow for where the hero will face, and hovering an enemy no longer shades its back.

- **V3.9, eyes front (2026-10-25 late night):** heroes and enemies see only what's in front of them. Enemies are blind behind; sleepers only hear. Each hero faces the way they last moved or struck, and Face (F) turns them for free; the party sees what any hero faces, so stand together to cover each other's backs. Hits from behind get an extra slot, and something you can't see striking from behind shows up as "Something strikes Pim from behind". The Burglar is the sneak: full-pace sneaking, quietest feet (5% to wake a sleeper, spotted at 2 when lit), and Backstab from behind on an unaware target for an extra slot and double damage, marked with a dagger when a Burglar is selected. The move preview only warns about enemies you've seen; walking into an unscouted room is on you. The free-swing confirmation and the shaded blind arcs are gone; facing shows as small arrows.

- **V3.7.2, back to turns (2026-10-25 night):** V3.8's exploration mode is shelved for later and the game is entirely turn-based again, including switching off V3.7's step-counting free move. The Lantern Sisters no longer come in by your way in: they use another entrance, arrive between rounds 6 and 9, and wait while you're near their door. Everything else from V3.7 and V3.7.1 stays.

- **V3.7.1, play-test fixes (2026-10-25):** flexibility with tradeoffs. Keven may go in alone: the camp opens on the notice post, the campfire glows as a suggestion, and the prepare screen warns "Keven goes in alone. If he falls, it's over." Starting gold is 90 (one level-1 hire and an item or two). The night-eye draught is Wim's: sold only once he is home, at the Gilded Flagon (the peddler shows it locked until then), bought into the stash. Lockpicks: every Burglar brings one improvised pick each delve (no pack slot; 2 Finesse slots, used up on any Pick); the smith's proper lockpicks (25) are kept and pick at 3 slots for a Burglar, 2 for anyone else; with neither, nobody picks (Smash, a charge or the skeleton key still open locks). Monsters notice a walking hero from 2 hexes farther (lit 8, unlit 5, the Hollow 8; behind them half that); sneaking is unchanged.

- **V3.7, stealth, free swings, the Sapper, crews that fight back (2026-10-25):** sneaking matters: walking makes noise at every hex and rolls to wake sleepers you pass (50% walking, 10% sneaking, less in the dark); awake monsters notice you at 6/4/3/1 hexes by light and pace; monsters face a direction and a sneaking hero on the hex behind them is never seen; your first hit on an enemy that hasn't noticed you gets an extra slot; the move preview shows every risk before you commit. Attacks of opportunity: leaving an enemy's side gives it a free swing, and your heroes swing at enemies that walk away. The Shadowmancer gets Dread (fear) and Night thorn (damage), the Hedgewitch gets Briar (damage and root), anyone with a bow gets Hamstring (root), front-liners can Brace, and the forge sells shields. Free movement: while nothing hunts you, click anywhere explored and the party walks there, the castle turning every 4 steps. The Sapper sets charges with a 1- or 2-round fuse: 4 damage within 1, a shove, 2 within 2, locks open, cracked and built walls break on Levels 1–2, floor next to a pit drops into it (bridges included), other floor turns to rubble; the Sapper starts with 3 charges and takes none from their own. Crates and barrels smash for loot. Rival crews are built like your squad with packs of your own items, focus their fire, rob and drag downed heroes to their exit, and a taken hero becomes a "Bring back" rescue contract (a taken Keven ends the campaign); the Wake waits in the Hollow. End turn only asks about heroes who did nothing. Saves start fresh.

- **V3.6, play-test fixes (2026-10-25):** the villagers are where they'd be: Brother Aldric in the chapel (taken on an herb run), Old Wim at the kitchen spit, Gerda barred inside the forge, the brewing copper among the barracks squad. Only the Burglar picks locks; anyone else smashes, unless they carry lockpicks from the smith (25). A night-eye draught (30) gives 8 rounds without dark penalties to sight or rolls (the Gloam still comes). Exits are marked "Way out" once seen, with a "Leave the castle" button when you stand on one, and the goal box points to the nearest way out once the prize is in hand. Heroes pass through hired crews.

- **V3.5, The Heir's Return (2026-10-25):** you play Keven, long-lost heir of the late Lord Hobart, camped in Lowgate, a derelict village under the castle walls. The village is won back one rescue at a time: Brother Aldric reopens the infirmary (until then, drink a potion from the stash), Gerda the smith relights the forge (smithy tiers, caltrops, grappling hook, skeleton key), and Old Wim turns the campfire into the Gilded Flagon (more and better recruits). Recover the brewing copper and Wim's cask book and the Flagon pours ales: one per hero per delve, each changing how that hero plays (never unlit, a free fall, digging, extra Focus, silent sneaking, healing on every level change, or the Black Cask's gamble). Story contracts are pinned on the notice post. Rival crews now parley: pay them to leave, buy a truce, hire them for a job or for 5 rounds of fighting (rich packs tempt them to turn), or fight; you pay with the coin and gems you carry. The Lantern Sisters still race you on Level 1; on Level 2 a cutthroat crew (the Ferrymen or the Gallows Choir) waits at a choke point and demands a toll. Saves start fresh.

- **V3.4, a tighter purse (2026-10-25):** a campaign starts with 120 gold and recruits cost 60 (level 1) or 100 (level 2), so your first choice is two hires and nothing else, or one hire and some cheap gear. Wounds carry over: heroes go back in with the HP they came out with, and the infirmary heals for 3 gold per HP (the old sit-out rule is gone). Falling into the dark shows only the lights below (a fire, the fungus, the pit glow), not the rooms they light, and standing in a castle light other than daylight lets you see 4 hexes (2 in the Hollow); your own lantern still sees farther. Hero cards are rebuilt: name and class, labelled HP, Focus and Mana, the pack as 4 sockets you can hover, and badges that explain themselves; every action, item, stage and counter has a tooltip with real numbers (Kindle: +6 lantern light for 1 mana). Torsos are shaped instead of boxes, walls stand at full height (heroes behind them show as a faint outline; a setting brings back the old lowered walls). Rival crews and a pub are written up as proposals, not built. Saves carry over.

- **V3.3, fear by depth (2026-10-24 night):** monsters are awake by default and some patrol (routes show once you've watched them walk); they notice you when lit or within 3, and give up after a 3-round search. The lantern holds 20 rounds and has a free **shutter**: Level 1 is bright enough to cross shuttered, Level 2 is a big disadvantage without light, and in the Hollow the Gloam rises the moment anyone stands in the dark and never enters light. Sight is per hero (in the dark: 4 hexes on Level 1, 2 on Level 2, 1 in the Hollow). Deeper monsters are tougher by tier. New: orc archers (knockback shots across the pit), orc captains (shield wall, rallying shout), ghouls buried in the Bone Pile (they grab), spiders web then drag. The Lantern Sisters now have a Ranger, a Shadowmancer and a Burglar using your own tricks. Figures re-proportioned to the chosen scale (option C). Saves start fresh.

- **V3.2, squad building and clarity (2026-10-24 evening):** a campaign starts with Lord Hobart alone and 160 gold; hire your squad at the tavern from eight classes (Sellsword, Burglar, Ranger, Sapper, Pyromancer, Shadowmancer, Hedgewitch, and Hobart). Dash: move again with your action, XCOM style. Twelve quartermaster items (smoke and glue bombs, sleep darts, caltrops, a grappling hook, a skeleton key, oil, firecrackers, plus the old four). Line of sight no longer leaves holes. The camera follows visible enemy actions during the castle's turn. Opened chests and ground loot glow until taken. Stairs and the lift are real models with a "Take stairs" button; contract objectives become beacons once seen; Take explains itself when the heirloom won't fit; every interactable says what you can do with it on hover; the HUD is regrouped with hotkeys and a "still waiting" count on End turn. Saves start fresh. Later the same evening: figures at Old School RuneScape scale (chunky, about two hexes tall), a Dragon about three and a half hexes long, and thin walls drawn along room edges while caves stay thick rock.

- **V3.1, the three-level castle (2026-10-24):** the castle from the approved map proposal (pass 2): the Ruined Keep, the Crossing (bridges over the pit) and the Hollow (caves, the Dragon's Lair, the Sealed Stair). Pits drop straight down (4 damage per level, 7 for two; two-level jumps ask first), shoves and railings make edges a weapon, orcs and berserkers push back, the Dragon breathes on marked hexes and sweeps with its tail, the winch runs the lift, sight shrinks with the lantern and with depth, and a 500-gold Dragon contract unlocks at reputation 3. New look: pale cutaway walls, floors per room, furniture, fixed lights, and you can see the levels below down the pit. Old saves start fresh.

- **Exploring by line of sight (2026-10-24):** every step of a walk reveals the layout of everything in line of sight within 8 hexes, lit or not, so long moves no longer leave gaps and walking into a room shows the room. Monsters still only show in light.

## Known gaps (V3.9.4)

- No touch or mobile pass.
- Monsters don't target hired crews; a cutthroat crew on a truce holds its post; a captive loses their non-valuable pack items.
- Balance: the bot finds Level 2 the difficulty wall; the sim's Burglar rarely backstabs.
- Later (docs/ideas-later.md): endless descent behind the Sealed Stair, fear and Nerve, companions, crafting and side quests, crews that remember you, arrows as ammunition, tripwires, ceiling collapse, a full economy pass.

## At launch (V3.0, 2026-10-24)

Kept as history; much of this has changed since (see above).

### The loop

Camp → take a contract from Hobart's bounty board → pick up to 4 heroes and their packs → delve → get out with the prize and whatever else you carried → spend it at camp.

- **Contracts:** Rescue (free a prisoner and walk them out) and Recover (carry an heirloom out). Level 1 jobs from the start; Level 2 jobs after your first success.
- **Lord Hobart** joins delves as the strongest hero. If he dies, the campaign is over.
- **Permadeath:** heroes at 0 HP are downed and bleed out in 2 rounds unless revived. Revived heroes sit out 2 contracts. The dead go to the memorial, and their gear stays where they fell.

### In the castle

- **Two hand-built hex levels:** the Ruined Keep (12 rooms around the great hall, the pit balcony, three exits) and the Barracks and Cells (cellblock, mess hall, locked storeroom, a cracked-wall shortcut). Preview (of the later three-level castle): docs/castle-preview/.
- **Turns:** each hero moves once and acts once, in either order; then the castle moves. Walk 4, or Sneak 2 quietly (the Burglar sneaks 4).
- **Slot rolls everywhere:** attacks, lockpicks, smashing doors, disarming traps. The preview shows slots, odds per hit count, modifiers and noise before you commit; Focus locks slots as hits.
- **Light is the clock:** a 40-round lantern. Bright, Dim and Dark make every roll harder; at pitch black the Gloam hunts you. Torches, braziers (they stay lit forever) and the mage's Kindle buy time. Splitting up is a real choice: heroes outside the light fight at Dark.
- **Noise:** every action makes a ripple you can see before acting. Sleeping monsters wake and come to look; closed doors muffle it.
- **Monsters show their intent:** who they'll hit and for how much. Rats, orcs (they shout for help), spiders (webs), the cutpurse (steals and runs) and berserkers.
- **The Lantern Sisters:** a rival crew that sometimes shows up to race you for chests and your prize. Leave them be, fight them, or bribe them 40 gold to leave.

### Camp

Bounty board, quartermaster (torches, potions, rope, blasting charges), smithy (weapon and armor tiers), tavern (recruits), infirmary, stash (sell valuables) and memorial.

### Presentation

Low-poly hex diorama with a rotatable camera (right-drag, `[` `]`), light and fog of war, roll animations, intents over enemies, event log, playback speed up to instant, hold Space to hurry the castle's turn, autosave. Keys: 1–4 select, Tab next, S sneak, E end turn.

### Known gaps at launch

No touch/mobile pass; Levels 3–5, the Dragon, the Sapper, other crews, hiring rivals and the endless Deep Vaults mode come later. Balance is a first pass from a bot: Level 1 is a warm-up, Level 2 is dangerous.
