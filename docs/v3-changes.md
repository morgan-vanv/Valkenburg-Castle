# What changed in V3: from Hobart's Revenge to The Heir's Return

V3 rebuilt the game as a turn-based extraction crawler, following the V3 Game Design Document (https://claude.ai/artifact/Q7766w5bBz11paXkw4YPf1). It launched as "Hobart's Revenge" (V3.0) and became "The Heir's Return" in V3.5. How the game works today: docs/current-rules.md. Rules with numbers, version by version: docs/v3-spec.md. V2 is in `main`'s history (last V2 commit 7a62311).

**Where it stands (V3.9.4, 2026-10-26):** Keven, heir of the late Lord Hobart, camps in Lowgate and wins the village back with story contracts; squads of up to 4 from eight classes delve a three-level castle (the Ruined Keep, the Crossing, the Hollow and its Dragon) on a 20-round lantern. Play is entirely turn-based. Stealth runs on facing and fields of view (V4.0: monsters see by their mood, heroes and crews are blind in a cone behind), noise and hearing; free swings both ways; the Sapper reshapes the level; rival crews parley, fight, rob and drag heroes off. Shelved: free exploration outside combat (docs/shelved/exploration-mode.md) and the Face command.

## Since launch

- **V5.3.1 (2026-10-29):** **Look closely at the keep.** Its highest window has gone dark, and something in it is looking back.

- **V5.3, the castle (2026-10-29):** **Valkenburg on the title is a figurine now,** built like the castle you delve: brick towers and walls on a crag of hex stone, lit by the moon. It has gone dark: a tower has fallen in, a wall is breached, the Hobart banner hangs in rags, and only a few windows still glow (they flicker; so do the lanterns on the path up). Mist drifts at its foot, and now and then a crow crosses the sky. **Lowgate shows your progress:** every villager you bring home lights a cottage window, and its chimney smokes.

- **V5.2, the title screen (2026-10-29):** **The first screen is new.** Valkenburg stands on its crag at dusk with Lowgate's fire below, and one card tells you where your campaign stands before you click: your heroes, your gold, who's home and who's in the graves; saved inside the castle, which level and round, and how much light is left. **Continue** (or Enter) picks it up. **New campaign** asks once before it buries a save, right there on the card. **Every run is remembered:** when Keven falls, the next start says how the last run ended and which run this is. **Settings and keys** are in the top corner, so you can set the quality or playback speed before you go in. The seed hides behind "Choose a seed".

- **V5.1.2, One for the road (2026-10-29):** **The ales are on the Party step now.** Under your party, the Gilded Flagon has two tabs: Hire and The taps. Every ale stands on its own tap in its own colour, with what it does in a line. Drag a tankard onto a hero (or click it, then the hero) and Wim pours; "Round" pours one for everyone going in who hasn't got one. Every hero card has a mug in its corner: click a full one (or the little tankard of a hero waiting on the roster) to **tip it back** for your gold (Wim's free round is a gift, and he'd be hurt). **Wim has a special every visit:** one ale 5 cheaper, chalked on his board. **Press Delve and the party raises a toast** before they go in, and a Black Cask cracks open in front of you so you see what it became (click, Space, E or Enter skips it; it never shows at the Instant speed). In the castle a drinker's card wears a little tankard: hover it for the effect; Featherfoot greys once its fall is spent, and a bad Black Cask shows red. Wim's tab in Outfit is gone; a dim "Wim's taps" pointer there takes you to them.

- **V5.1, the camp (2026-10-28):** **Lowgate is redrawn and nothing scrolls.** The camp is one lit tabletop, scaled to fit any screen: hex steps across the top, your gold as a growing stack of coins, the wagon and the graves as buttons in the corner. **Job:** six cards at most, pay as coins, risks as marks, the story on hover. **Party:** your heroes as figurine busts with an icon for every ability; drag someone from the roster or the Flagon into an open slot (a recruit is hired as they land). **Outfit:** drag a ware onto a hero's pack to buy it and pack it in one go, or press Buy to fill the hero marked "Buying here"; the wagon now only holds spares and loot. Locked wares wait in a strip under the shelf, saying how to unlock them. **Set out:** the castle map is drawn in hexes from what you remember, with the stairs marked, the job sealed in red and your way in glowing. **The graves** are a night yard outside the north gate: every name cut in the old gatepost, a headstone and candle for the fallen. Anything you can drag you can also click or do from the keyboard. **The figurines appear instantly:** the hero busts are drawn ahead of time, so opening the camp after a delve no longer hangs for a moment.

- **V5.0, the look (graphics track, 2026-10-28):** **The castle is a diorama now.** Warm light, soft bloom from every flame, a tilt-shift blur that keeps the party sharp and lets the edges of the board fall away, a vignette; the camera sits closer (16) and lower, tilting down as you zoom out and flattening as you zoom in; the right mouse button still turns it freely. **Figures are figurines:** about a hex and a third tall with big heads, built on a joint rig that walks, sneaks, swings, draws a bow, casts and flinches; packs and bedrolls on their backs, a torch in the hand of whoever carries one; every hero class, the orcs, archers, cutpurses, ghouls, rats, the spider, the Gloam, the Lantern Sisters with their lanterns, the crews in their colours, the prisoner and the Dragon. **Walls are brick:** straight runs along the hexes with stone pillars at the corners, rock in the caves; the wall between you and your party drops to a stub so nothing hides behind it. **Floors are bevelled tiles** with gaps between them. **Every room is dressed:** banners and the Hobart crest in the hall, bookcases and scroll chests in the library and archive, chains and skulls in the cells, anvils in the forge, mushrooms and roots in the Hollow. **Statuses show on the figure** (the play track's spec): a hero in the dark goes cold and blue (the black ring is gone), poison tints green, a downed hero lies in a red pool that grows as the count falls, a hunting monster's hex has a red rim, a brace glows gold, a veil goes violet, a blocked hit flashes the shield. **The action cam** pushes in on every blow, shot, spell and throw and hands the camera straight back. **Settings:** Quality (Low turns off shadows, bloom and tilt-shift for older machines), Action camera, Tilt-shift.

- **V5.0 (the review, 2026-10-28):** **More on the figure:** a Sellsword's Guard draws a gold line on the floor to the friend it covers; a Ranger on overwatch stands in a pale breathing ring; a warded hero's edge glows gold-green; a hero who is held, rooted or webbed (and a glued monster) gets a thin ring in the cause's colour inside any hunting ring; a dozing hero dims; an unlit hero's tag says **unlit** (red, "the dark hunts", in the Hollow). **The shield flash plays** on a block (it had been built but never shown). A second, harder hit from the same enemy asks about the block again instead of reusing your first answer. The Stairs button says the step across is free; the block card says the Focus left after the block. The Gloam warning steps above a first-delve tip instead of hiding under it.

- **V5.0 (play track, 2026-10-28, after the first play-test):** **Captions go away on their own** a few seconds after the roll (the "X gets a free swing at Y" card had stayed up until the turn ended). **The Walk / Sneak switch is tidier**, with the Shift hint beside it instead of squeezed in. **Settings has the look rows:** Quality High / Low, Action camera, Tilt-shift blur; "Lower walls near the camera" is gone (walls near the camera are always cut away now).

- **V5.0 (play track, 2026-10-28):** **The stairs.** The step across is free, so a hero gets on, across and off in one move (sneaking too), and nobody standing on the far step blocks the next one down. **You can look down the stairs:** standing on them shows the landing below within three hexes, light permitting, with an eye mark on the card and a line in the log saying what's there ("orc on patrol, 2 hexes from the foot"). What's there can see you on the stairs too, and a hunter will climb — unless you hold the step. While the rest of the party still has to come down, the view stays on the level you're looking at. **A toll you can pay.** A cutthroat crew now asks for half of what you carry (50 at least, but never more than you have); if you carry nothing they look you over and let you by: "we'll see you on the way out." They mean it. **Swinging at a crew you're at peace with now warns you** in the roll panel ("The Ferrymen are not your enemy yet: this makes them hostile, and they keep what you paid"), and if you paid them a toll or a bribe the panel asks first, like a long jump does. The rule hasn't changed: any attack makes them hostile and they keep the coin.

- **V5.0 (play track, 2026-10-27):** **Buying puts things on Keven's cart, nothing else.** The cart row glows; click it to pack the thing for the hero marked Packing, or use the little "Give to" buttons under it to hand it to anyone in the party. Forge work and ales are still for the hero you've picked. **E confirms.** With a roll panel or a question open (a long jump, "they've done nothing this round"), E, Enter and Space all confirm it; E ends the turn only when nothing is open. The reaction card still wants 1 / Enter or 2 / Esc, so a stray E never strikes from hiding for you. **The roll panel's bar is thicker and speaks for itself:** the kill chance sits on the kill segment (with what it was before Focus), nothing under the bar, and every segment tells its full number on hover. **The turn has a flow now.** When a hero has nothing plausible left (no move, nothing to attack, take, open or use), the camera moves on to the next hero after a moment; while they can still loot the chest they opened or step through the door they opened, it stays with them. The question before End turn only comes when someone can still do something, and says what. When nobody can, the turn ends itself (Settings → Auto end turn). Any key or click keeps things where they are. **Status marks on the party cards:** braced, guarding, guarded, overwatch, veiled and warded each have their own mark now; a downed hero's bleed count is big and red; a poisoned hero's HP bar ends in green; the log says when someone first stands in the dark. **The Wake is a little weaker** (level 5): a party of four at level 4 wins about half the time. **The return report counts up.** Back in Lowgate the haul lands first (the strongbox counts up), then the party's XP fills and level-ups punch in, then, for a job done for the village, a full card: who's home, what reopened and the gift they press on you; then what you left behind (chests seen and shut, loot seen and not taken), then the castle. Click, E or Space skips to the end. **Rangers:** an arrow at something that has seen you draw loses a try; unaware targets take the full draw and the sneak-attack bonus as before (crew archers too). **The shield blocks:** with the forge shield and a Focus to spend, a hit that would hurt asks: take it on the shield (1 Focus, 1 damage) or take it; crew Sellswords block hard hits too. **New keys.** W A S D pan the camera (the arrows still do); F centers on your hero; Space attacks (or takes the marked target); a tap of Shift flips the new Walk / Sneak switch for the selected hero; E still ends the turn and now confirms anything open; `?` shows every key. Disarm and Dig moved to N, charges and the winch to C. **A downed friend's pack can be looted** (Take, next to them: everything but their kit), and a downed crew member's too; **a potion works on the captive you're rescuing** when they're hurt. **Jumping into a pit is your move, not your whole turn.** You land with your action ready: light a torch (the Hollow is no place to stand dark), drink, attack, take. No dash after a jump; you land hard. Climbing a rope or the hook still takes the whole turn.

- **V4.1, reactions (2026-10-27):** **The castle's turn can stop and ask you.** When an enemy steps past a hero it hasn't spotted (or one who's veiled) and that hero's action is ready, the turn pauses: "The orc steps past Fenna, who's hidden from it. Strike it, or let it pass?" **Strike** takes the free swing, and gives Fenna away (a veil breaks, and the orc turns on her, hit or miss); **Let it pass** keeps her hidden. A Ranger on overwatch is asked too, when something moves in sight: **Shoot** it now, or **Hold** for another (still armed for the rest of the castle's turn). Answer with the buttons, or 1 / Enter and 2 / Esc. Don't want to be asked? Each hero has a small **Reactions** button beside their name: set each reaction to Ask, Always or Never, and the castle never stops for it. Leave the game mid-question and it asks again when you come back. An enemy that has already seen a hero still eats the swing as before.

- **V4.1, squad size (2026-10-27):** **You start with 70 gold:** enough for one hire and a torch, or a few things for Keven alone. **A party is three heroes to begin with.** The fourth slot shows locked in Lowgate's Party step: Wim says the garrison's old **muster roll** still lies in the officers' quarters on Level 2, behind the locked door where the Lantern Sisters camp. Once your renown reaches 2, the job to fetch it goes up on the post (it pays no gold and brings no gift); bring it home and a fourth hero can go in with you for good. Campaigns already under way keep their four slots. **Contract XP is shared now:** a finished job is worth 8 XP split among the heroes who got out (8 for one, 4 each for two, 3 for three, 2 for four), so a small band levels faster; the return report says "Contract: 8 XP shared by 2." Kills, getting out and the Dragon are as before. **Crews come as many as you are:** the Lantern Sisters, the Ferrymen and the Gallows Choir field two to four, matched to the number of heroes you take in; the Wake never fewer than three. The Level 1 and 2 crews each have a new member for a party of four (Sister Maud, a Hedgewitch; Ferryman Kees, a Burglar; Verger Crane of the Gallows Choir, a Shadowmancer), and a smaller crew leaves its extra hands behind but always brings its best: the Sisters keep the Burglar with the keys and their archer, the Ferrymen their Sapper, the Choir Little Mags and her poison dart.

- **V4.1, play-test fixes 2 (2026-10-27):** **A killing blow from the shadows stays quiet:** if your hit kills something that hadn't seen you, it no longer flashes "Noticed!" as it drops; anything else nearby still hears the fight and the body fall. **Free swings are stricter:** moving at all from a hex next to an enemy that's hunting you (backing off, circling round it, slipping past) gives it one free swing, once per move however many steps you take; stepping up to it is still free. The move preview shows the swing before you click. It works the other way too: an enemy that moves around you while your action is ready eats your swing, and crews and monsters play by the same rule. **Unshackling needs a lock tool:** lockpicks, a Burglar's improvised pick or the skeleton key. No more smashing the chains; without a tool, carry the prisoner out. **The job board has two rows:** the village's pins (pleas from Lowgate, and your own heroes a crew took) above the work for hire, three at most in each.

- **V4.1, fixes (2026-10-27):** a waiting crew on Level 2 no longer comes after you because an orc shouted at them while you were still upstairs. The guard by the Level 2 stairs now walks to the arcade instead of through the barracks, so the sleeping squad stays behind its door unless you open it.

- **V4.1, crews are your equals (2026-10-27):** a rival crew now fights the way your party does. **Pick a fight with an even crew and it's a coin toss; pick one while a level behind, a hero short or worn down from the delve, and expect to lose.** What they do, named in the log: they **steady for the kill** (Focus) when a blow can drop you; they **flank** (+1 slot with a crewmate behind you) and walk round to do it; their Sellsword **braces** when two of you are on him; they **drink or hand over antipoisons**; their Shadowmancer puts monsters to **Sleep**, fills a hero with **Dread** (no action next turn, though you can still move) and looses **night thorns**; their Hedgewitch calls up **briars**; a hired Pyromancer **kindles** your torch. Their mages walk to where a spell reaches and cast from out of your reach; nobody runs in alone if they can wait for the others; their archers like a raised perch. A crew that's losing badly **calls the fight off** and falls back, carrying its own (strike them after and they fight to the end); while half of them are down, the parley card offers a **truce or a way out for nothing**. Neutral Sisters who see a much stronger party **keep their distance**. On Level 2 a crew waiting at its post keeps its **lights hooded**: you won't see them until they speak. Crews are tougher across the board (the Sisters and the Ferrymen level 4, the Gallows Choir 5, the Wake 6), and your fireball now respects a crew member's armor.

- **V4.1, arrows (2026-10-27):** **The Ranger has a quiver now:** 5 arrows a level, their own, fresh every delve (a level 2 Ranger brings 10). It sits beside the pack slots as "➶ 10" and counts down with every shot: attacks, overwatch, knockback, hamstring, even a free swing as something runs past. **Mott sells plain arrows** (10 gold for 6) for when the quiver runs dry, and if it does, the Ranger pulls a knife: next to the target only, 2 tries, 1 damage. **Specialty arrows:** when you aim with some in the pack, pick the arrow above the bar (plain by default; the `,` and `.` keys step through them), and the roll panel says which one you're loosing. **Whistling** (Mott, 15 for 3) shrieks where it lands to draw monsters off; **glow** (Ilse, 20) lights the spot or sticks in what it hits and lights it up wherever it goes, and the Gloam hates it; **fire** (Ilse, 25) hits harder and sets the ground burning; **poison** (Ilse, 30) poisons; **barbed** (Gerda, 25) pins a runner in place; a **rope arrow** (Gerda, 30) ties a rope at a pit's edge from across the room; **blast arrows** (Gerda with Ilse home, 40 for 2) burst like a small charge, blowing locks and walls. Whistling, glow, rope and blast arrows can also be shot at any spot you can see, from their pack slot. **Ilse's homecoming gift** now includes 3 glow arrows. **Crews shoot by the same rules:** their Rangers carry quivers too and run out; the Lantern Sisters' Ranger carries glow arrows and lights up a hero lurking in the dark, the Ferrymen's carries barbed arrows for a hero trying to make off.

- **V4.1, poison and the downed (2026-10-27):** **Venom and paralysis are gone; poison replaces them.** A spider's bite, a **poison dart**, a **poison trap** or Little Mags' dart leaves you **poisoned**: you lose 1 HP every turn, but never your last one, until you cure it with an **antipoison** (yours or a friend's, even while you're down), a Hedgewitch's **Mend**, or by getting out. Spider-milk Stout keeps it off you for a delve. **A downed hero bleeds out in 3 turns now** (was 2), and the count shows on their tag and card. **Revive is gone:** a potion from next to them gets them up at 5 (a greater potion at 8), a Hedgewitch's Mend from 3 away at 2. Or **Carry** them: lift a downed friend (an action) and they ride on your shoulders, on your hex, not bleeding; you move 3 and do only free things (doors, take, give, leave) until you set them down, and walking out with them gets them home alive at 1 HP. Anything that hits your hex hits them too. With Gerda and Aldric both home, **splints** let a downed hero crawl a hex a turn to an exit. **Prisoners come shackled:** carry them out, or **Unshackle** them with lockpicks, the skeleton key, an improvised pick, or by smashing the chains (loud); unshackled, they follow you without slowing you down. **Crews play the same way:** a crew member at 0 goes down instead of dying, and their friends will potion them up, mend them, or carry them out; finish one off and their loot drops. A crew carrying off one of your heroes now carries them, too.

- **V4.1, the castle teaches (2026-10-27):** two orcs now guard the tower stair down (a firecracker in the passage sends both looking); the servants' passage is dark, trap and all; the hall's west orc stands on the pit's edge, waiting for a shove. On Level 2, Ugluk sleeps by Gerda's forge (go quietly), the archers' perches give +10 to whoever holds them, and Grub the Jailer hears the winch.
- **V4.1, the Outfit screen (UI, 2026-10-27):** step 3 is two counters now, so you always know whether you're buying or packing. **On the left, "Yours":** each hero's card with their four pack slots (the one you're packing for says "Packing"), the kit they bring of their own, and what they already have from the village (blade and mail pips, a shield, the ale poured), then **Keven's cart** right underneath: pack gear from it, sell valuables one at a time or all at once. Nothing on this side has a price except what a valuable is worth. **On the right, "Vendors":** a tab each for **Mott's wagon, Gerda's forge, Ilse's bench and the Flagon's taps**, always all four. Each row has the item, a line on what it's for, how many you already have, its price, and a button with the price on it ("Buy · 20", "Forge for Fenna · 60", "Pour for Fenna · 15", "Free · a gift" for Gerda's shield). What you buy goes straight into the selected hero's pack (the slot glows), or onto the cart if their pack is full, and the row tells you which. Hover anything for the numbers. **For play-testing, every vendor lists everything they will ever sell:** things you can't get yet are greyed out with a lock, and hovering one gives you a hint about who in the castle might make it. A villager who isn't home yet still has a tab, saying so ("The forge is cold.").

- **V4.1, torches are the clock (2026-10-27):** **No more free lantern.** You go in with whatever light you pack. **Torches** (Mott, 10) light 3 hexes round whoever holds one for 15 rounds; lighting one takes the action, **Snuff** is free and keeps what's left, and **Drop torch** leaves it burning on the floor to light the spot (pick it up again later). Several heroes can carry a lit torch each. **Gerda's lantern** (120, once she's home) holds 20 rounds, lights 4 (3 when it runs low), has a free shutter, and is refilled free every time you come home; lose its bearer and you lose it. Ilse's **oil** adds 10 to it, and the Pyromancer's **Kindle** lights or tops up a friend's torch or lantern from 3 hexes away. **Darkness is where you stand:** in your own light or daylight you see as far as ever and roll at full; lit only by a sconce, a fire or fungus you see 4 (3, 2 deeper) and roll −10; in the dark you see 4 / 2 / 1 and roll −20. **The Gloam** only stirs on Level 3, for heroes standing unlit. **Night-eye Stout** at the Flagon lets the drinker see in the dark all delve (old night-eye draughts on the cart are bought back for 15). **Crews carry their own light**, so you see them coming; a hostile crew goes for whoever carries the most light. The Lantern Sisters leave by round 18. **Level 1 has dark rooms now:** the library, the tower and the armory lost their sconces, so their chests sit in the dark. The top of the screen shows every lit torch and lantern with who carries it and the rounds left; job cards say **Unlit** when you remember a site no castle light reaches; Set out warns when nobody packs a light.

- **V4.1, gold, gifts and Ilse (2026-10-27):** **Jobs that win something back pay no gold any more.** Rescuing a villager, recovering the copper or the cask book, or bringing back one of your own from a crew pays nothing; instead, the first time each villager comes home they bring a gift: Brother Aldric two potions, Old Wim a round of Hobart's Reserve for everyone on your roster (drunk as they next go in), Gerda a free shield at the forge, Ilse two antidotes. The job card shows the gift where the gold was. **Regular jobs pay more:** rescues 110 / 200 / 280, recoveries 140 / 250 / 340 (plus the heirloom), the Dragon 600. **You start with 120 gold**, and on your very first delve the orc guarding Aldric in the chapel is dozing: a chance to learn to sneak and backstab. **Ilse the alchemist** is a new villager: once Aldric is home and word has spread, he asks you to fetch her from the archive on Level 2, where she has lived among the shelves beside the spider. Smoke and glue bombs, sleep and venom darts and oil are hers now; Mott keeps torches, potions, antidotes, rope, firecrackers and the blasting charge (now 45), and no longer sells the night-eye draught. **Villagers work together:** with Wim and Ilse home, three new brews at the Flagon (Night-eye Stout, Spider-milk Stout, and Dragonfire Porter, which takes 2 off fire, burning, dragon breath and blasts); with Gerda and Ilse, blasting charges at the forge for 30 and the Sapper's **tripwire** (a charge that goes off the moment an enemy steps next to it, or when its fuse runs out); with Aldric and Ilse, the **greater potion** (35, heals 8) and antidotes for 12; with Aldric and Wim, a **hot supper**: everyone who gets out heals 2.

- **V4.1, other ways in (2026-10-26):** the Lantern Sisters never come in by the way you did. Go in by the sally port or the east breach and they may come through the main gate behind you.

- **V4.0, play-test fixes 2 (2026-10-26):** **The Sapper** is a breacher, not a second Sellsword: no more Brace, and a little lighter (HP 9, Might 60, no armor; the Sellsword keeps 10, 70 and his armor). Sappers you already have come down to the new numbers (a wounded one keeps their wounds), and so do the crews' Sappers. **Free kit grows with the hero:** a Sapper brings one improvised explosive a level each delve (a new Sapper brings 1, not 3), a Burglar one improvised pick a level, and each Pick uses one up; the card's kit mark shows how many are left, and Lowgate's Outfit says "Brings their own: 2 improvised picks". Crews follow the same rule. **Picking a target is clear:** with an action armed, the option under the mouse gets a bright white ring with a coloured halo, its tag lights up and the others dim; a jump or shove shows where it lands, a throw or fireball the area it hits. The list above the bar marks its target on the board when you hover an entry, says which way each same-named one is ("Orc · east"), and Tab or the arrows step through the options, Enter takes the marked one. **The hotbar shows only what the hero can do now:** no dimmed Attack, no Set charge without charges; with nothing to do it says "Nothing to do here. Move, or End turn." and the name's hover lists what they can do elsewhere. The bar never scrolls (labels fold to icons if it would). Pack items say "Click to use (key H)" instead of the old "Items ›" menu path. **The Set out sketch is bigger:** it fills the column, names grow with it, and the lantern choice sits under the plan.

- **V4.0, venom (2026-10-26):** spiders' bites are venomous now, on every level. A hero who is bitten (or pricked by a venom dart, or who steps on one of the two new venom traps on Level 2, ringed in green once spotted) is **envenomed**: a green drop with a number appears on their card, the turns until they are **paralyzed**. Paralyzed, they can't move or act for 6 turns, though they don't bleed and aren't down; hits still hurt, and monsters still come for them. Cure it with an **antidote** (Mott always has them, 20 gold: drink it, or give it to a friend next to you), with the Hedgewitch's **Mend**, or by getting out of the castle. Or carry them: next to a paralyzed friend, **Shoulder** lights up; they come along on every move (you move 3 at most), and if you leave by an exit they come out with you. A paralyzed hero left inside when the delve ends is lost. Mott also sells **venom darts** (30 gold): a silent Finesse throw that envenoms a monster or a crew member, who locks up three turns later. The Gallows Choir's Little Mags carries one too, and saves it for a hero standing alone.

- **V4.0, taken only when alone (2026-10-26):** being carried off by a crew is now the rare, nasty surprise, not the usual end of a crew fight. A crew takes a hero only when they are **helpless and alone**: downed, or knocked out by a crew Burglar's sleep dart, with no friend on their feet within 4 hexes. Stay together and a crew that downs one of you finishes them instead if nobody is standing over them; stand next to the crew member and it fights you rather than finish your friend. Wander off alone and a Burglar's sleep dart may find you first: seized while out cold, you wake in their grip being hauled toward the way out, and can try to break free (2 Might slots, 1 hit) or fight back, while the others come running. A lone Keven can be taken too, and that still ends the campaign. And a paid "Open a lock" job that ends with a jammed lock is just a failed job now: the crew keeps your money.

- **V4.0, the board is the screen (UI, 2026-10-26):** the in-castle HUD is rebuilt in Lowgate's look. Along the top: **the lantern** (its stage, a short line such as "daylight: shut it to save oil", the round, and a notched bar of 20 in its bright, dim and dark bands; click it or press L to shut or open it, hover for the numbers), **the job** (what, where, "not yet seen" or "in sight", and a compass arrow once you know where it is; after the prize is in hand, the way out), **Carrying** (what the party carries, e.g. "100 · 45 gold, a garnet": a crew's toll is half of it, and hired crews may turn on a party carrying 150 or more), the **levels** by name once found ("Keep", then "?" until you find the next), and the menu. ## At launch (V3.0, 2026-10-24)

Kept as history; much of this has changed since (see above).

### The loop

Camp → take a contract from Hobart's bounty board → pick up to 4 heroes and their packs → delve → get out with the prize and whatever else you carried → spend it at camp.

- **Contracts:** Rescue (free a prisoner and walk them out) and Recover (carry an heirloom out). Level 1 jobs from the start; Level 2 jobs after your first success.
- **Lord Hobart** joins delves as the strongest hero. If he dies, the campaign is over.
- **Permadeath:** heroes at 0 HP are downed and bleed out in 2 rounds unless revived. Revived heroes sit out 2 contracts. The dead go to the memorial, and their gear stays where they fell.

### In the castle

- **Two hand-built hex levels:** the Ruined Keep (12 rooms around the great hall, the pit balcony, three exits) and the Barracks and Cells (cellblock, mess hall, locked storeroom, a cracked-wall shortcut). Preview (of the later three-level castle): docs/castle-preview/.
- **Turns:** each hero moves once and acts once, in either order; then the castle moves. Walk 4, or Sneak 2 quietly (the Burglar sneaks 4).
- **Slot rolls everywhere:** attacks, lockpicks, smashing doors, disarming traps. The preview shows slots, odds per hit count, modifiers and noise before you commit; Focus locks slots as hits.
- **Light is the clock:** a 40-round lantern. Bright, Dim and Dark make every roll harder; at pitch black the Gloam hunts you. Torches, braziers (they stay lit forever) and the mage's Kindle buy time. Splitting up is a real choice: heroes outside the light fight at Dark.
- **Noise:** every action makes a ripple you can see before acting. Sleeping monsters wake and come to look; closed doors muffle it.
- **Monsters show their intent:** who they'll hit and for how much. Rats, orcs (they shout for help), spiders (webs), the cutpurse (steals and runs) and berserkers.
- **The Lantern Sisters:** a rival crew that sometimes shows up to race you for chests and your prize. Leave them be, fight them, or bribe them 40 gold to leave.

### Camp

Bounty board, quartermaster (torches, potions, rope, blasting charges), smithy (weapon and armor tiers), tavern (recruits), infirmary, stash (sell valuables) and memorial.

### Presentation

Low-poly hex diorama with a rotatable camera (right-drag, `[` `]`), light and fog of war, roll animations, intents over enemies, event log, playback speed up to instant, hold Space to hurry the castle's turn, autosave. Keys: 1–4 select, Tab next, S sneak, E end turn.

### Known gaps at launch

No touch/mobile pass; Levels 3–5, the Dragon, the Sapper, other crews, hiring rivals and the endless Deep Vaults mode come later. Balance is a first pass from a bot: Level 1 is a warm-up, Level 2 is dangerous.
